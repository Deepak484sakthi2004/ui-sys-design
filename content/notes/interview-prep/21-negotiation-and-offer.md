# Chapter 21: Negotiation & Offer

> **Relearning log.** The thing I'd forgotten — or never properly learned — is that **the interview
> and the negotiation are two different games, and most engineers win the first and lose the
> second** by accepting the first number out of relief. The recovery: negotiation isn't
> adversarial begging; it's a structured, low-emotion conversation where **information and optionality
> are the leverage**, and the highest-leverage variable isn't comp at all — it's **level**. I'm
> writing this down now, before any offer, because the calm strategy has to be decided *before* the
> dopamine of an offer call clouds it. (Target: 90+ LPA, India — see
> [Ch 1 comp context](01-levels-loops-and-rubrics.md).)

---

## 21.1 The comp components (know what's negotiable)

| Component | Negotiability | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|-------|
| **Base salary** | Low–medium | Bounded by level bands; modest movement |
| **Equity / RSU** | **High** | The big lever at senior levels; 4-yr vest. Where 90+ LPA comes from |
| **Sign-on / joining bonus** | **High** | One-time cash; used to bridge gaps or offset unvested equity left behind |
| **Level** | **Highest leverage** | Moving up one level dwarfs any single-component negotiation |
| **Relocation, benefits** | Low | Usually fixed |

> **Total comp (TC), not base, is the number that matters.** In India at senior levels, RSUs are
> often the largest slice. Always compare offers on **TC over the vest period** (and on the
> *steady-state* annual once fully vested), not on base alone.

---

## 21.2 The single highest-leverage move: level

A higher level changes the *entire band* — base, equity, future raises, and the ceiling — far more
than negotiating RSUs at a fixed level. So:

- **Aim the whole loop at the higher level** ([prepare to the L5 bar](01-levels-loops-and-rubrics.md))
  — leveling is decided by interview performance, mostly the *scope* you demonstrated in design and
  behavioral.
- **If down-leveled,** ask what specifically was missing and whether a follow-up/extra round could
  re-evaluate. Sometimes a borderline result can be revisited.
- **Competing offers can justify a level review,** not just a comp bump.

> If I have to choose where to spend negotiation energy: **level first, equity second, sign-on
> third, base last.** Level compounds for years; a sign-on is one-time.

---

## 21.3 The strategy (calm, information-driven)

1. **Never accept on the call.** "I'm really excited — thank you. Can you send the details in
   writing? I'd like to review and get back to you by [date]." Buys time, kills the dopamine
   decision.
2. **Don't anchor yourself first.** When asked "what are your expectations?", deflect early: "I want
   to focus on fit; I'm confident you'll make a competitive offer for the level. What range did you
   have in mind?" If pressed, give a *researched, ambitious-but-credible* range, not a single number.
3. **Get competing offers if you can.** Optionality is the strongest, most honest leverage. Run loops
   in parallel so timelines overlap. A real competing offer reshapes both level and comp.
4. **Negotiate on TC and level, with a reason.** "Based on my level, scope, and competing offers,
   I'm targeting a TC of ₹X. Can we close the gap on equity or a sign-on?" Tie the ask to market and
   to the value you bring.
5. **Be specific and collaborative, not adversarial.** Name the component and the number. "If we can
   get the sign-on to ₹X to offset the equity I'm leaving, I'd sign this week." Give them a clear path
   to yes.
6. **Use the recruiter as an ally.** They want to close you; help them justify a higher number to
   their comp committee with concrete data (competing offer, market range, your level).

## 21.4 India-specific notes

- **RSU vesting & currency:** US-listed RSUs vest in USD-denominated stock; value swings with the
  stock price and INR/USD. Model TC at a *conservative* stock assumption, not the peak.
- **Vesting schedules vary:** some are 25/25/25/25 over 4 years, some front- or back-loaded; ask for
  the exact schedule — it materially changes year-1 vs steady-state TC.
- **Refreshers & cliffs:** ask about annual equity refreshers (they offset the year-4 vesting cliff)
  and any 1-year cliff on the initial grant.
- **Joining bonus** is commonly used in India offers to bridge a gap or buy out a notice period /
  unvested equity — explicitly negotiable.
- **Notice period / buyout:** Indian roles often have long notice periods; a sign-on can cover a
  buyout — raise it.
- **Compare apples to apples:** Indian startups may quote ESOPs with uncertain liquidity vs.
  liquid public RSUs — discount illiquid equity heavily when comparing.

## 21.5 Evaluating beyond the number

Comp is necessary but not sufficient. I weigh:
- **The team and the problem** (you join a team, not a logo) — growth, tech, manager.
- **Level and trajectory** — title and scope set your next 2–3 years.
- **Equity upside vs. liquidity** — public RSUs vs. startup ESOPs.
- **Work-life and on-call reality.**

> The best offer for a 25-year-career engineer optimizes **scope and growth**, not just this year's
> TC. A higher level on a stagnant team can be worse than a slightly lower TC on a team that
> stretches you. I'll hold both in view.

---

## 21.6 Common pitfalls

- **Accepting immediately** out of relief → leaving money and level on the table.
- **Anchoring yourself first** with a low number.
- **Negotiating base only** and ignoring the much larger equity/sign-on levers.
- **Bluffing a competing offer** you don't have — risky and can backfire; use real optionality.
- **Comparing on base instead of TC over the vest** — apples to oranges.
- **Being adversarial** — it sours the relationship you're about to enter; stay collaborative.
- **Ignoring level** — the one variable that compounds.

## Interview Drills

- **D21.1 [E]** List the comp components in order of negotiability and say which compounds most over
  time. *(Level > equity > sign-on > base.)*
- **D21.2 [M]** Script your response to "what are your salary expectations?" without anchoring
  yourself.
- **D21.3 [M]** You get L4 at ₹78 LPA but targeted L5/90+. Write the two-sentence ask you'd make and
  to whom. *(Level review with the recruiter, backed by scope/competing offers.)*
- **D21.4 [H]** Compare a ₹95 LPA public-RSU offer vs a ₹1.2 Cr startup-ESOP offer — list the
  factors and how you'd discount the ESOP.

## Key Takeaways

1. **The interview and negotiation are different games;** decide the calm strategy *before* the offer
   call.
2. **Level is the highest-leverage variable** — it compounds for years; equity is second, sign-on
   third, base last.
3. **Never accept on the call; don't anchor yourself first; get real competing offers** for honest
   leverage.
4. **Negotiate on total comp over the vest**, tied to level + market + value; be specific and
   collaborative, recruiter as ally.
5. **In India, model RSUs conservatively, ask for the exact vest schedule and refreshers, and use
   sign-on to bridge gaps** (incl. notice-period buyout). Optimize scope and growth, not just this
   year's TC.
